Starting the game

SHIFT-BREAK, you know the score.

Configure your game from the main screen.

Move the red cursor with the arrow keys, and press any key (except "R" or f0) to toggle through the options.

Press "R" to randomise the ship loadouts, to add a bit of variety to your competitions. This won't change the controls or winner, just the ship configurations.

When you are happy, press f0 to play.

The default configuration is for one human player: it's an evenly matched fight between two Cobra Mk IIIs, with player 1 (human) on keyboard and player 2 (AI Pilot) on auto-pilot. In essence, this configuration is similar to dogfights in the original Elite, except the game shows the view from the enemy ship as well as the human player. The computer controls the player using the same tactics code as in the original game, though the computer's ship has the same shields and energy level as the human player, so it will take a lot longer to kill than a typical in-game enemy (as the in-game enemies have very weak shields and energy levels in comparison).

See the end of this document for the full list of game options.

Controls: Player 2

Player 2 can be configured to be an AI Pilot, or to use Joystick or Delta 14B.

For the AI Pilot, the computer controls the player using the same tactics code as in the original game, though the AI's ship has the same shields and energy level as the human player, so it will take a lot longer to kill than a typical in-game enemy (as the in-game enemies have very weak shields and energy levels in comparison).

For Joystick, the primary flight controls are on the stick (pitch, roll, fire lasers), and the following controls are on the keyboard:

The missile keys have been placed in a row so the fire button is furthest away from the right-hand side of the keyboard, which is where player 2 will have to sit when sharing the keyboard with player 1.

For those lucky enough to be playing on a BBC Master, player 2's keyboard controls are also available via the numeric keypad, as follows:

In this instance the missile keys are reasonably logical: there's "+" and "-" to switch the missile's targeting system on and off, and when you have target lock, you can press the explosion-like "*" key to fire the missile. You can also activate your E.C.M. to DELETE any enemy missiles.

The view keys are also logical, and both a diamond and WASD-style layout is supported. The arrow keys also work, which some people might prefer.

For the Delta 14B, all controls are on the stick; see below for details.

Controls: Delta 14B

If you have a Delta 14B/1 adaptor box and a Delta 14B joystick, then you can play the game without needing the keyboard... and if you have a Delta 14B/1 adaptor box and two Delta 14B joysticks, then both of you can play the entire game using only your sticks.

See this PDF for details of the keys and which sockets to use.

The Delta 14B option will only work if you have a 14B/1 fitted. You can use your Delta 14B as a normal joystick without the 14B/1 fitted, in which case choose Joystick (the fire button will work, but the other buttons won't).

Space views and dashboards

Player 1 plays in the top space view, using the left set of dials. Player 1 should look for cyan blobs in the compass and scanner.

Player 2 plays in the bottom space view, using the right set of dials. Player 2 should look for yellow blobs in the compass and scanner.

The dials should be familiar to anyone who has played the original:

LabelMeaning
SPSpeed
ACAttitude control (this combines the RL and DC dials from the original game)
FSFore shield
ASAft shield
LTLaser temperature
ENEnergy level (this combines the four bars of the original game into just one bar)
MissilesGreen = unarmed, Yellow = targeted, Red = locked

Each player has their own compass. This points at the other player, so you can track down your enemy should they go off-scanner. A thick dot means the other player is in front of you, while a thin dot means the other player is behind you.

The 3D scanner is shared between both players.

Player 1 should look for cyan blobs on the scanner, as these show player 2 and any missiles that player 2 has launched. Just ignore the yellow.

Player 2 should look for yellow blobs on the scanner, as these show player 2 and any missiles that player 2 has launched. Just ignore the cyan.

If you forget which colour you should be looking for, just glance at your compass, as this uses the colour you should be looking for.

Ships are shown with two-pixel high heads on the sticks, while missiles are shown with one-pixel high heads.

Pause options

Press COPY to pause the game and DELETE to resume.

While paused, the following option keys from the original game are supported:

KeyOption
SSound on
QSound off
CAPS LOCKToggle keyboard damping
AToggle keyboard auto-recentre
FToggle flashing console bars
YReverse joystick Y-channel
JReverse both joystick channels

When two joysticks are being used, the last two settings will apply to both joysticks.

Notes

  • In terms of gameplay, the choice of ship type only affects the targetable area: larger ships are easier to hit. Handling and shields are the same with all ship types.

  • In all games, you can win by reducing your opponent's energy levels to zero, just like the original game. In hit-based games, you can also win by reaching your target hit count before your opponent. Each laser strike counts as one hit, as does each missile strike. Beam and military lasers rack up hits in a continuous manner, while pulse and mining lasers are much slower.

  • Each player can have a maximum of one missile in-flight at any one time; you will get a "missile jammed" message if you try to launch a second missile before your first one has exploded. Note that you must wait for the explosion cloud to dissipate before you can launch another missile.

  • Collisions between players will damage both ships, but the faster ship gets a much higher penalty.
